## Notes

- SMD is the standardized mean difference (MCI minus CN); |SMD| > 0.1 is the
  usual threshold for a non-negligible imbalance.
- p-values are unadjusted. Six tests are reported; at a Bonferroni alpha of
  0.0083 none of the conclusions change.
- Continuous variables use Welch's t-test except APOE e4 allele count (a 0/1/2
  ordinal) and MMSE (which piles up at the 30-point ceiling); those use
  Mann-Whitney U. Both p-values are in `table1_cn_vs_mci.csv` for every
  continuous row.
- MMSE is not an independent predictor here: a low MMSE is part of how the MCI
  diagnosis was made, so its separation is partly definitional. This is why the
  clinical-only baseline (Model 1) is expected to lean almost entirely on it.
- Age barely separates the groups (p = 0.21), so it contributes little to the
  clinical-only baseline.
- Sex is the largest categorical imbalance in the table: 59.5% of CN are female
  against 37.6% of MCI (SMD -0.45). Model 1 therefore has a second real
  predictor besides MMSE, and any protein that tracks sex will look predictive
  for reasons unrelated to disease -- worth checking against the Phase 4 SHAP
  hits.
- ADNI phase is tabulated because phase and diagnosis are confounded in this
  cohort: ADNIGO enrolled early-MCI participants only, with no new controls.
  Any phase-level correction is therefore partly a correction for diagnosis.
- This section is hand-written. `scripts/02d_eda_stats.py` regenerates the table
  above on every run but preserves everything from the `## Notes` heading down,
  so edit it freely -- and re-check the numbers quoted here if the cohort ever
  changes.
